LLM safety is not one property. It is two.

Safety benchmarks test one phrasing at a time. If a model refuses one wording and complies with a semantically identical rewrite, nothing catches it. I built a benchmark to expose that gap across production models.

Source

Holding meaning constant, moving only the words

329 adversarial seed prompts grounded in 14 MITRE ATT&CK techniques, expanded to 1,645 variants through four controlled paraphrasing strategies: lexical substitution, logic reversal, syntactic restructuring and contextual reframing. Every variant means the same thing. Only the wording moves.

Five agents, no human in the loop

Five autonomous query agents each called a different production LLM API, with independent rate limiting, structured JSON output parsing and fault-tolerant error recovery. They ran in parallel and processed 8,225 requests without human intervention.

Scoring used a three-judge G-EVAL consensus: each AI judge independently evaluated every response and voted by majority to produce a final safety verdict. 24,675 structured verdicts in total.

The result I did not expect

I proposed the IIR metric, Intent-level Inconsistency Rate, to measure how often a safety decision changes across semantically equivalent phrasings of one intent. Strict inconsistency came out at 60.2%.

Then the models that scored best on safety turned out to be the worst on consistency. The model with the highest mean safety alignment (MSA 59%) had the highest inconsistency rate (IIR 72.6%).

Safety alignment and behavioural consistency are independent properties. A single safety score tells you how often a model refuses, not whether it refuses the same thing twice. Both matter, and only one of them is usually measured.

Models evaluated

Claude, GPT-4o, DeepSeek, Mistral and Qwen, each through its own production API.

The thesis ships with full reproducibility documentation.
